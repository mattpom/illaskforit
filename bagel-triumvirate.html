The True Bagel Triumvirate

Not all bagels are created equal. There are three. Only three. And a lot of places selling you "bagels" are actually selling you dessert cake shaped like one.

Words by Sean Matthews · Food & Drink · One Man's Opinion

Before we start, let's define what a bagel actually is. A bagel is a hand-rolled ring of yeasted wheat dough that is boiled and then baked. It has a dense, chewy crumb, a shiny crust from the boiling, and it weighs something. When you pick it up, you should feel it. If you pick up a bagel and it feels like a pillow, that's not a bagel. That's a disappointment shaped like a bagel.

This distinction matters because a lot of places have successfully convinced large portions of the American public that a soft, bloated, sweetened ring of bread is the same category of food as what you get at a real bagel place. It is not. Panera is the worst offender here — what they hand you in a bag is not a bagel, it's dessert cake that got shaped like one and boiled just enough to earn the name. If Indianapolis wants to know what a real bagel tastes like, go to Bagel Fair. That's the gold standard. Everything gets measured against that.

With that foundation established, here are the three. The True Bagel Triumvirate. These are the only correct choices when faced with a menu at a place that actually makes real bagels. Everything else is a supporting player.

#1
Plain Bagel
The purist's choice, and the correct #1. A plain bagel has nowhere to hide. No seeds, no salt, no garlic doing the work for it — just the crumb, the chew, and the crust from the boil. This is the bagel that actually tells you whether a place knows what it's doing. Order the plain first at any new spot. If it's not right, nothing else on the menu is going to save it.
#2
Onion Bagel
The one people sleep on. Toasted onion flakes baked into the crust give you sweetness and a little bite without turning the whole thing into a production. It's more interesting than plain without trying to be the loudest thing on the menu. An underrated bagel for an underrated reason: it actually tastes like something.
#3
Everything Bagel
Still elite. Still correct. The seeds and garlic and onion and salt create a crust that adds a second layer of texture to an already textually interesting food. It lands at #3 in this triumvirate for one reason only — it doesn't test a bakery the way plain does. You don't need to be creative when you order the everything. You just need it to actually be good, which, at a real bagel place, it is.

A note on what's not on this list: poppy seed, salt, sesame, pumpernickel, blueberry, cinnamon raisin, whole wheat, and everything else. These are not bad things. Some of them are good things. None of them are in the triumvirate. The triumvirate is not a list of all acceptable bagels. It is a list of the three bagels that a serious bagel person would order every time without thinking too hard about it.

And to be clear — if what you're eating came from a bakery known for sandwiches and pastries and it's soft enough to fold in half without resistance, you're not eating a bagel. You're eating a sweet roll that got boiled for appearances. That's a Panera problem, not a bagel problem.

↑ The Verdict

PLAIN. ONION. EVERYTHING. IN THAT ORDER.

The True Bagel Triumvirate has been established. The criteria are clear: density, crust quality, topping contribution, and overall purpose on this earth. Any bagel that cannot justify its own existence against these three is merely a shaped bread product doing its best. If you want to know what "justify its own existence" actually looks like, go to Bagel Fair. Order accordingly.
