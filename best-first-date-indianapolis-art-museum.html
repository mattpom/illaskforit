The Best First Date in Indianapolis Is the Art Museum

Dinner tells you what someone orders. A museum tells you whether you can stand listening to them have an opinion.

Words by Sean Matthews · Indianapolis · One Man's Opinion

The best first date in Indianapolis is the Indianapolis Museum of Art at Newfields.

Not dinner. Not drinks. Not coffee under lighting normally reserved for police interviews.

The art museum wins because a first date is not supposed to determine whether two people can split an appetizer. It is supposed to answer a more important question: Is this person interesting to walk around the world with?

At the museum, you start finding out immediately.

"A first date over dinner can turn into a job interview with bread."

Sean Matthews · One Man's Opinion

Where did you grow up? What do you do? Do you have siblings? What are you looking for? Why did your last relationship end? By the time the entrées arrive, both people have accidentally completed a background check.

The museum gives you something better to discuss than yourselves. Every gallery throws another conversation starter directly at you.

What catches your date's attention? What do they walk past? Do they look at the art before reading the label, or do they require written instructions before experiencing a rectangle?

Can they explain why they like something without saying, “I don't know, it's just cool”? Can they dislike something without announcing that modern art is a scam perpetrated by people who own scarves?

This is useful information.

"Put them in front of a painting they do not understand and let's see what happens."

The Critical-Thinking Test

Critical thinking does not mean knowing the difference between Impressionism and Post-Impressionism. Nobody should be eliminated from romantic consideration because they cannot identify a brushstroke from 1887.

It means they can look closely, form an opinion, explain it, hear your opinion, and survive the discovery that the two of you do not agree.

If your date says, “I don't like it, but I can see why you do,” that is a good sign.

If they say, “Anyone who likes this is an idiot,” congratulations: the museum just saved you the cost of dinner.

Your date does not need to appreciate every piece. Some art is beautiful. Some is confusing. Some appears to have been assembled from items found behind a hardware store. The point is not agreement. The point is curiosity.

Do they stop when something surprises them? Do they ask what you see? Can they admit they do not understand something? Can they change their mind after looking for another minute?

Curiosity improves travel, food, conversation, relationships, disagreements, and ordinary Tuesday afternoons. It is also more revealing than learning that both of you enjoy tacos. Everyone enjoys tacos. That is not compatibility. That is functioning taste buds.

"The museum fixes a basic first-date flaw: staring directly at a stranger for ninety minutes."

Why Newfields Works

At Newfields, you walk side by side. You look at the art. Silence does not mean the date has died. It can simply mean you are both looking at the same thing and neither person feels compelled to fill every second with a story about work.

If a gallery is not working, move. If a conversation becomes interesting, sit down. If the date is going well and conditions cooperate, the broader Newfields campus gives you room to keep it going.

And if the date is not going well, museums have exits. Beautiful, clearly marked exits.

There is one rule: do not turn this into a secret cultural-literacy examination. You are not a professor. Your date is not defending a thesis. Nobody receives extra points for recognizing the artist before reading the wall label.

The museum works because both people reveal themselves naturally. While you are noticing whether your date is curious, thoughtful, dismissive, playful, or pretentious, your date is collecting the same information about you. Fair is fair.

Also, check Newfields' current hours, admission details, exhibitions, and visitor information before you go. “I thought they were open” is not mysterious spontaneity. It is poor planning.

↑ The Verdict

THE ART DOES THE HEAVY LIFTING.

The best first date in Indianapolis is the Indianapolis Museum of Art at Newfields. It creates surprise, disagreement, laughter, silence, and hundreds of chances to answer the real first-date question: How does this person see the world? If you like the answer, then go get dinner.
