Why Geist Reservoir's Painted-Poster Treatment Actually Works

A covered pontoon, a plain concrete dock, and a reservoir that isn't a real lake -- here's why turning Geist Reservoir into a vintage travel poster captures Indiana summer better than a photo ever could.

Words by Sean Matthews · Deeper Dive · Worth the Trip

Geist Reservoir is not a lake. It is a flood-control reservoir, dammed out of Fall Creek back in 1943, and calling it "Lazy Summer Days" material is a small act of branding. Somehow it works anyway.

That is the trick of this whole "Painted Memories" treatment. It borrows the old WPA travel-poster formula -- thick oil-paint strokes, a stately serif title, a clean location line at the bottom -- and points it at something that was never built to be a postcard. No canyon. No mountain range. Just a plain concrete dock, a shrink-wrapped pontoon, and a row of houses that could be anywhere north of Indianapolis.

National park posters ask you to be impressed. This one asks you to remember something. Those are two different jobs, and the second one is harder to pull off.

The covered boats are doing more work than they look like they're doing. A tarped-up pontoon is not really a summer image. It is a between-summers image, the thing you walk past in April or October on your way to check on the dock. Putting it front and center is the artist admitting that most of what we remember about lazy summer days is not the day itself. It is the anticipation sitting on either side of it.

Then there is the dock, doing exactly what a good composition needs a dock to do. It pulls your eye straight down the middle of the frame and out toward the water, the same walk a few thousand Indianapolis-area kids have made barefoot on hot boards without thinking twice about it.

And "Indiana, USA" printed along the bottom, with the same deadpan confidence you would give "Amalfi Coast, Italy," is the whole joke and the whole tribute at once. Nobody is flying in for this particular view. That is not a knock on it. Plenty of the places that made us are places nobody would vacation to on purpose.

There is also something honest about choosing a reservoir over a natural lake in the first place. Geist exists because Indianapolis needed drinking water and flood control, not because a glacier carved out something scenic ten thousand years ago. The houses, the docks, and the boats all showed up after the fact, built by people who decided a utility project could still be worth a summer. That is a very Midwestern kind of optimism, and the painting treats it with more dignity than the origin story technically earns.

So here is the actual question worth sitting with: what is the unglamorous dock, reservoir, or backyard in your own life that would hold up if somebody put a coat of oil paint and a fancy title card on it?
