Journey Is Not Journey Without Steve Perry

There are things you can replace in a band. The drummer. The bassist. The keyboard player nobody remembers. You cannot replace the voice. Steve Perry was not a member of Journey — Steve Perry was Journey.

I'll Ask For It  ·  One Man's Opinion  ·  Music

Let's start with the facts, because the facts are not in dispute. Journey, formed in San Francisco in 1973, went through several lineup changes and musical identities before Steve Perry joined as lead vocalist in 1977. What happened next is the entire argument.

In the four years between Perry joining and the release of Escape in 1981, Journey became one of the highest-grossing concert acts in the world. "Don't Stop Believin'." "Open Arms." "Faithfully." "Any Way You Want It." Every song you know from Journey — every song that comes on at a wedding, at a bar, in a movie where someone is trying to feel something — was recorded with Steve Perry singing it.

"Steve Perry was not a member of Journey. Steve Perry was Journey."

The Voice Argument

There are voices in rock and roll history that define their bands so completely that the band becomes unthinkable without them. Freddie Mercury and Queen. Robert Plant and Led Zeppelin. Steve Perry and Journey. These are not interchangeable.

The counterargument, usually delivered by someone who wants to seem fair-minded, is that "the music is still good." And sure. The musicians in Journey are excellent. Neal Schon is a genuinely great guitarist. But when you hear Arnel Pineda — the current lead singer — the experience is one of recognition followed immediately by wrongness. It's the song you know. It's the melody you know. And it's not him.

This is not a criticism of Pineda, who is objectively talented and who was doing karaoke Justice before he was discovered on YouTube. The problem is not his voice. The problem is that he is not Steve Perry, and Journey is Steve Perry.

The Counterargument, Addressed

"But bands replace members all the time." Yes. And sometimes they remain the same band. AC/DC replaced Bon Scott with Brian Johnson, and that transition worked because Johnson brought something — a different energy, a different presence — that allowed the band to remain itself in a new form. The band was bigger than one voice.

Journey was not bigger than one voice. Journey was one voice. You can test this yourself: listen to the pre-Perry albums. They're fine. They're a competent rock band doing its thing. They are not the band that filled arenas for a decade. The voice is the thing.

"Everything that followed is a cover band with better equipment."

Why This Matters

It matters because music history gets distorted when we allow the brand name to outlast the thing that made it mean something. "Journey" now refers to a touring entity that performs songs Steve Perry recorded. That touring entity is good at what it does. It is not Journey.

The songs hold up because Steve Perry made them. The arena-sized emotional impact of "Don't Stop Believin'" comes from a specific voice hitting specific notes with a specific kind of conviction that you don't develop — you either have it or you don't. Perry had it. No one after him has had it in the same degree.

This is not a complex opinion. It's just one that nobody says out loud at a wedding when the DJ plays "Open Arms" and everyone sings along to the recording of a man who hasn't been in the band since 1996.

The Verdict

Journey Without Steve Perry Is a Tribute Band That Owns the Name. Full Stop.
